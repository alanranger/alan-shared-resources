## 2. Non-negotiable editorial principles

- **Teach judgement, not recipes.** Emphasise seeing, choice, intent, composition, review. Avoid universal settings and slider values.
- **Do not duplicate live tuition.** Lightroom, detailed camera operation and image-specific critique will be taught on Zoom using Constantin's own photographs.
- **Do not over-specify the trip.** Time, weather, transport, family schedule and light are unknown. Provide flexible prompts, not rigid shot instructions.
- **Do not turn the PDF into a form.** Constantin is unlikely to type into the PDF. Remove repeated worksheets and blank writing pages.
- **Use Alan's own voice and work.** Any image/quotation must have a clear educational purpose.
- **Quality over page count.** Tighter and more useful, not longer.

**MASTER FILTER:** Before adding anything, ask *"Will Alan teach this better live over Zoom?"*
If yes, keep only minimum context in the handbook and leave the detailed teaching for the session.

## 8. Page-by-page Version 3 specification

**Cover (p1)** — Retain title, Constantin's name, dates, Alan's credentials. Replace plain cover
with one strong relevant Alan photograph or restrained architectural treatment. Premium and
calm, not a travel brochure. No taglines/badges/levels/excess text.
Asset: `assets/workbook-images/01-cover/constantin-nz-cover.jpg`.

**Welcome (p2)** — Rewrite opening only. Shift from 'holiday itinerary' to a structured
photography project and development opportunity. Direct, encouraging, personal. Don't demand
notes/paperwork before discussions. Keep safety, curiosity, honest engagement.

**How This Works (p3)** — Retain concise mentoring loop (learn, practise, photograph, review,
improve, repeat). Introduce the **30-50-10-10 Vision Framework** as the central thinking model.
Distinction: mentoring loop = how learning develops over time; Vision Framework = how to think
while making each photograph. Reduce duplicated Dropbox/booking instructions. No second
competing methodology. Asset: `03-vision-framework/alan-30-50-10-10-framework.svg` or `.png`.

**Your Field Kit (p4)** — Rename from 'Your Kit'. Keep Canon body, lenses, tripod, monopod,
iPad/Lightroom. Shift from product inventory to decision-making; briefly say when each item is
useful and when unnecessary. Avoid specs and "carry everything". Prefer a small **'Choose for
the situation'** callout. Principle: 24–70 for responsive general work; 100–500 for
isolation/compression; tripod only when the image needs stability or long exposure.

**Meet Your Mentor (p5)** — Keep strongest bio/philosophy; reduce CV-style credentials and
marketing. Emphasise why images work, honest critique, repeatable decisions. Add **one authentic
teaching photograph** + **one short pull quote**. First choice image: existing RPS/online

on-location. Path: `02-mentor/alan-teaching.jpg`. Suggested quote: *'Understanding why a picture
works is what allows you to do it again.'*

**Before You Fly / Academy prep (p6)** — Keep linked Academy prep. Trim table if cramped. Clearly
split **Essential vs Recommended**. Retain UK bridge/building practice assignment. Don't add
modules for the sake of it. Keep links functional. Explain *why* each selected lesson matters for
this project.

**Auckland overview (p7)** — Keep route overview, three-bridge structure, safety warning. Reframe
around three engineering eras / three photographic opportunities. Add one mentor tip: don't

**Grafton Bridge (p8)** — Keep concise history/engineering (shortened). Replace 'What to

## 10. Image-use rules

- Every image must have a defined purpose: inspire, establish authority, illustrate a visual principle, or demonstrate Alan's thinking.
- No generic stock photography. No decorative fill. Don't repeat the same Alan image on multiple pages.
- Teaching example → add a **40–80 word 'Why this photograph works'** caption. Analyse composition, emphasis, timing, visual organisation, feeling; don't list EXIF/settings unless essential.
- **Max two or three analysed Alan photographs** in the whole handbook.
- Careful crops; don't distort aspect ratio or crop out the point.
- Record source page, original filename, downloaded filename and local path in the asset inventory.

## 11. Lightroom and editing scope — ONE PAGE MAXIMUM

Handbook does not teach Lightroom. Keep only the mentoring workflow: review all → identify
strongest → first edit → upload → review on Zoom → refine. No masks, curves, colour grading, AI
tools, export tutorials, slider values or control screenshots. Alan teaches Lightroom live via
screen sharing using Constantin's own photographs.

repeated Dropbox/booking instructions on multiple pages · repeated slogans / duplicated

## 13. Tone of voice

Direct, intelligent, encouraging, honest. Written to a capable 16-year-old; never childish or
patronising. Practical and experienced. First person selectively in mentor notes and personal
pages. Avoid corporate jargon, inflated claims, generic motivational language. Avoid overusing em
dashes, rhetorical flourishes and repeated slogans.

- **Good:** *'Look first. Decide what the photograph is about. Then choose the lens and settings that help you say it.'*
- **Avoid:** *'Unlock your creative potential through an exciting photographic journey of discovery.'*
